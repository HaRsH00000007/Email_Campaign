Email Campaigning Platform

Simple User Guide

A practical guide to uploading your leads, writing outreach emails, sending them from your own Gmail accounts, and tracking who replies — no technical knowledge needed.

Written for marketing and outreach teams
No coding required

1. What this tool is

A workspace for running cold-outreach email campaigns from start to finish.

You bring a list of contacts and the message you want to send. The platform sends that message to each person individually from your own Gmail account, spaced out over time so it looks like normal human sending rather than a mass blast.

Then it watches your inbox for you. When someone replies, the platform notices, marks that person as interested, and automatically stops sending them follow-ups — so nobody is ever chased after they have already answered.

What it does for you

  • Personalises every email — any column in your spreadsheet (first name, company, job title) can be dropped into the message.
  • Tests two versions of a pitch against each other, and sends polite follow-ups to people who did not reply.
  • Tracks the outcome of every single email — delivered, replied, wrong address, out-of-office.
  • Exports everything to a spreadsheet whenever you need it.

What it deliberately does not do

Worth knowing up front, so you are not waiting for something that will not arrive:

  • No open or click tracking. You will not see who opened the email or clicked a link. Replies are the only signal of interest.
  • No built-in unsubscribe list. The platform will not automatically stop emailing someone who asks to be removed — see the best-practice section, because this one matters legally.
  • Gmail accounts only as sending addresses.

2. Getting started

Opening the platform

Your administrator will give you a web address to open in your browser. On a machine set up for local use this is usually http://localhost:3000. There is nothing to install — it runs in the browser like any other website.

Signing in

  1. Open the address. You land on a Sign in card.
  2. Enter your email address and password, then press Sign in.
  3. If you have not been given an account yet, press Create an account and fill in your name, email, and a password of at least 10 characters.

After signing in you land on the Mailboxes page, because connecting a sending account is the first thing that has to happen before any campaign can go out.

3. Finding your way around

There are only three places to go. They are listed down the left-hand side of every screen.

SectionWhat it is for
Campaigns Every campaign you have created, with its live results — how many were delivered, how many replied, and your reply rate. This is your home base once things are running.
Lead lists Your contact lists. Upload a spreadsheet here and it becomes a list you can point a campaign at.
Mailboxes The Gmail accounts your campaigns send from, and the daily and hourly sending limits for each one.

Your own email address and a Sign out button sit at the bottom of the same panel.

4. Working with leads

A lead list is simply a spreadsheet of people you want to contact. You upload it once and reuse it across campaigns.

Preparing your file

  • Save it as CSV or Excel (.csv, .xlsx or .xls).
  • Put the headings in the first row.
  • There must be a column called email. Without it the upload cannot work.
  • Add any other columns you want to use in the message — first name, company, job title, city, and so on.

Every column becomes a personalisation field. If your sheet has a column called firstName, you can write {{firstName}} in your email and each person sees their own name in its place.

Uploading

  1. Go to Lead lists.
  2. Give the list a clear name. Q1 SaaS founders is far more useful in six weeks than list 2.
  3. Choose your file and press Upload.
  4. A progress bar shows the import. Large files take a moment.
  5. When the status turns to ready, the list can be used.

The table then shows the list name, how many leads it holds, and which columns were found. Check that column list, because those are exactly the personalisation fields available to you.

Some rows are skipped on purpose. Anyone with a missing or malformed email address, and any address that appears twice in the same file, is dropped during import. If the final count is lower than your spreadsheet's row count, that is why — and it is a good sign, not a problem.

How to organise your lists

  • One list per audience, not one giant list. A message written for heads of talent should not go to the same list as one written for founders.
  • Keep lists small and focused. Relevance beats volume every time in cold outreach.
  • Clean the file before uploading. Fix blank first names — an email that opens “Hi ,” is worse than one with no name at all.

5. Creating a campaign

Press New campaign on the Campaigns page. A five-step guide walks you through it, and you can move back and forth freely before anything is sent.

Step 1 — Mailboxes

Tick the Gmail account or accounts this campaign should send from. If you tick several, the platform rotates between them so each stays under its own daily limit. Spreading volume across a few mailboxes is much safer than pushing one hard.

Step 2 — Leads

Choose which lead list to email. Underneath, the platform shows the exact personalisation fields available from that list — worth a glance before you start writing.

Step 3 — Templates

Name the campaign, then write your email. This is the step that matters most, and it is covered in detail in the next section.

Step 4 — Schedule

Decide how fast to send. There are two modes:

ModeHow it behaves
Spread
recommended		 Your list is spread evenly across a number of days. You choose how many days, how often a batch is released, and a random gap between individual emails (for example 60–180 seconds). The randomness is the point — it looks like a person typing, not a machine firing.
Fixed rate Sends continuously at a set rate. Faster, but noticeably more mechanical. Use it only when you have a reason to.

In Spread mode a short summary shows how many emails per batch, per day, and per mailbox per day your settings actually produce. If a warning says sending will run behind schedule, your gaps are too long for the volume — shorten the gap, lengthen the interval, or add another mailbox. This step also holds the Rewrite each email with AI option, explained next.

Step 5 — Review

A summary of every choice you made: mailboxes, list, number of pitch variants, follow-ups, pacing. Check it properly — this is the last screen before real emails go to real people.

Watch for the personalisation warning. If you used a field that does not exist in your lead list — {{firstname}} when the column is actually {{firstName}} — a warning lists it here. Left unfixed, that spot arrives completely blank in every email. Go back and fix it.

Finally, choose:

  • Save as draft — saved, nothing sent. Safe.
  • Save and start sending — the campaign goes live and sending begins on your schedule.

6. Writing your emails

Subject and body

Each email has a subject line and a body. Write them as you would write to one person, and use personalisation fields wherever a detail should change per recipient. Read the result aloud before you send it — if it sounds like a template, it will be treated like one.

Hi {{firstName}},
I noticed {{company}} has been hiring quickly this quarter…

Testing two versions (A/B)

Press Add variant to write a second version of the pitch. Each lead is randomly given one of them, which splits your list roughly in half. After a few days, compare the reply rates and keep writing in the style of the winner.

Follow-ups

Most replies to cold outreach come from the second or third email, not the first. Leave Send follow-ups to people who do not reply ticked and add one or two steps:

  • Set how long to wait after the previous email. Three days is a sensible starting point.
  • Leave the subject line blank and the follow-up attaches to the original email thread, which reads far more naturally than a fresh email.
  • Keep it genuinely short. Two or three lines.

Follow-ups stop the moment someone replies. This is automatic and cannot be forgotten. Nobody is chased after they have answered you.

The AI helpers

There are two, and they are separate:

FeatureWhat it does
Draft with AI
(Step 3)		 Describe your campaign in a sentence — intro for a recruiting tool, aimed at heads of talent — and press Draft 3. Three draft emails appear; press Use this on one to load it into the editor, then edit it yourself. It is a starting point, never a finished email.
Rewrite each email
(Step 4)		 Your template becomes a reference, and each recipient gets a slightly reworded version of it — same offer, same call to action, same links, different phrasing. This helps avoid every recipient receiving an identical email.

Both AI features must be switched on by your administrator. If drafting does nothing, or rewriting seems to have no effect, that setup has not been done — ask your admin. Rewriting is designed to fail safely: if it cannot run, or the result looks wrong, your original wording is sent instead. A campaign never stalls because of it.

7. Mailboxes — your sending accounts

A mailbox is a real Gmail account that your campaigns send from. Emails genuinely come from that address, and replies land in that inbox exactly as normal.

Connecting one

  1. Go to Mailboxes and press Connect Gmail.
  2. Google asks you to sign in and approve access.
  3. Accept every permission requested. If you skip the read permission, the platform cannot see replies — which means follow-ups will keep going to people who have already answered you.
  4. You are returned to the platform and the mailbox appears as connected.

This step may not be available yet. Connecting Gmail requires a Google configuration your administrator has to complete first. If pressing Connect Gmail shows an error, that setup is outstanding. It is not something you can fix from the interface — contact your administrator.

Sending limits

Each mailbox shows how many emails it has sent today and this hour against its limit, and you can set both limits yourself.

The single most important setting in this tool. Gmail technically permits a high daily volume, but sending anywhere near that from a cold-outreach account will land you in spam folders and can get the address blocked.

Start at 30–50 per day. If you need more volume, connect more mailboxes rather than raising the limit. A brand-new address should start lower still — 5 to 10 a day, built up over several weeks.

The mailbox card also warns you if a permission is missing or if Google reported an error. Both are fixed by disconnecting and reconnecting the account.

8. Monitoring your campaign

Open any campaign from the Campaigns list to see its results. Figures refresh automatically while the page is open.

What each result means

ResultMeaning
QueuedWaiting its turn. Not sent yet.
DeliveredReached a real inbox. This is the number your reply rate is measured against.
RepliedA genuine human reply. Follow-ups to this person have stopped.
Wrong addressThe address does not exist. Remove it from your source list.
BouncedA temporary problem — mailbox full, server busy. May succeed later.
Auto-replyOut-of-office or similar. Counts as delivered, but not as a reply.
FailedCould not be sent. Usually a permission or limit problem on the mailbox.

The buttons you will use

  • Pause / Start — stop or resume sending at any time. Pause immediately if anything looks wrong.
  • Check replies — fetch new replies right now instead of waiting for the next automatic check.
  • Export CSV — download every recipient and outcome as a spreadsheet.
  • Click any recipient row to read the full conversation with that person: what you sent, and what they wrote back.

What to check after launching

  1. Within the first hour: is anything actually being delivered? If every row says Failed, pause and speak to your administrator.
  2. Day one: how many say Wrong address? More than a handful means the list is stale. Pause and clean it — continuing damages your sending reputation.
  3. Every day: filter to Replied and answer those people personally. This is the entire point of the campaign.

9. A workflow that works

Prepare your lead file ↓
Upload it and check the column list ↓
Connect a mailbox and set a safe daily limit ↓
Create the campaign ↓
Write the pitch and follow-ups ↓
Set pacing — spread over several days ↓
Review, and fix any field warnings ↓
Test on 10–20 leads first ↓
Launch to the full list ↓
Check results daily ↓
Reply personally to everyone who responds

Never skip the test run. Upload a small list of 10–20 contacts — including your own address — and send to that first. A broken personalisation field or an awkward sentence costs you nothing on 20 people, and costs you the whole list on 2,000.

10. Best practices

  • Keep lists clean. Remove old and bounced addresses. A list full of dead addresses damages your ability to reach the live ones.
  • Send less than you can. 30–50 a day per mailbox. Volume comes from more mailboxes, never from a higher limit.
  • Be relevant. If the message would not make sense to the person receiving it, they are on the wrong list. Irrelevant email is what gets marked as spam.
  • Reply fast. Interest fades quickly. Check replies daily and answer the same day where you can.

Staying on the right side of the law

Cold outreach is regulated — GDPR in the UK and EU, CAN-SPAM in the US, and similar rules elsewhere. In practice:

  • Include a clear way to opt out in your email text, and honour it.
  • Say who you are — a real company name and a real postal address.
  • Only contact people with a legitimate business reason to hear from you.

Opt-outs are handled by you, not by the platform. There is no automatic unsubscribe list in this tool. When somebody asks to be removed, you must delete them from your source spreadsheet yourself so they are not included in any future upload, and reply to confirm. Missing this is the most common compliance mistake, so put a routine around it from day one.

11. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Cannot sign in Check the email and password. New passwords must be at least 10 characters. If it still fails, the platform itself may not be running — ask your administrator.
Signed out unexpectedly Your session expired. Sign in again.
Upload rejected, or the list is empty The file almost certainly has no column named email. Check the first row of the spreadsheet and re-upload.
Fewer leads than rows in my file Expected. Invalid addresses and duplicates within the file are skipped automatically.
Import status says failed Delete the list, check the file opens correctly as CSV or Excel, and upload again.
“No usable mailbox” in the campaign wizard No Gmail account is connected, or the connected one lacks send permission. Go to Mailboxes and connect or reconnect.
Connect Gmail shows an error Google access has not been configured for this installation. This is an administrator task and cannot be resolved from the interface.
“Read permission missing” warning A permission was skipped during connection. Replies cannot be detected, so follow-ups will not stop. Disconnect the mailbox, reconnect, and accept everything Google asks for.
Campaign is active but nothing sends Check, in order: the mailbox is connected; its daily or hourly limit is not already reached; the campaign is not paused; and, in Spread mode, that the next batch time has actually arrived. If all of that looks fine, ask your administrator to check the sending service.
Everything says Failed Pause the campaign immediately. This points at the mailbox connection rather than your content. Reconnect the mailbox and read its error message.
Lots of Wrong address The list is out of date. Pause, clean the list, and re-upload. Continuing to send harms your sending reputation.
AI drafting does nothing The AI service has not been configured by your administrator. Everything else works normally without it.
No replies at all after several days Not a fault. Review the subject line, whether the message is genuinely relevant to that audience, and whether the list is well targeted.
